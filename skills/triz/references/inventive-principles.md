# TRIZ 40 Inventive Principles

Each principle is a generalized solution strategy. When applying them, always translate the abstract principle into concrete ideas for the user's specific domain.

---

## 1. Segmentation
Divide an object or system into independent parts; make it sectional for easy assembly/disassembly; increase the degree of fragmentation.

*Examples: modular furniture, microservices architecture, segmented solar panels*

---

## 2. Taking out (Extraction)
Separate an interfering part or property from an object, or extract only the necessary part or property.

*Examples: extract the noise source from the workspace; use remote sensors instead of embedded ones*

---

## 3. Local quality
Transition from homogeneous structure to heterogeneous; make each part function in conditions most suitable for it; make each part fulfill a different and useful function.

*Examples: graduated bicycle helmet padding; different soil zones in agriculture; multi-layer coatings*

---

## 4. Asymmetry
If an object is symmetrical, introduce asymmetry; if already asymmetrical, increase the degree of asymmetry.

*Examples: asymmetric tire treads for better traction; one-sided packaging for easier opening*

---

## 5. Merging
Bring closer in space the same or similar objects or operations; assemble identical or similar parts to perform parallel operations; make operations contiguous or parallel instead of sequential.

*Examples: combine multiple functions in one device; parallel processing; co-locate related services*

---

## 6. Universality
Make a part or object perform multiple functions; eliminate the need for other parts.

*Examples: Swiss Army knife; smartphone replacing camera/GPS/music player; a sofa-bed*

---

## 7. Nested doll (Russian dolls)
Place one object inside another; pass one object through a cavity in another; increase the number of nested layers.

*Examples: telescoping antenna; folding umbrella; data compression (files within archives)*

---

## 8. Anti-weight (Counterweight)
Compensate for the weight (or undesirable effect) of an object by merging it with another that provides a lifting or opposing force.

*Examples: counterweight in elevators; ballast in ships; using buoyancy to offset weight*

---

## 9. Preliminary anti-action
If it is known that an object will experience harmful stress, apply counter-stress in advance to offset the future harm.

*Examples: pre-stressed concrete; preventive medication; pre-loading springs*

---

## 10. Preliminary action
Perform required changes to an object completely or partially in advance; arrange objects so they can come into action from the most convenient position.

*Examples: pre-cut vegetables; pre-filled forms; Just-In-Time preparation; caching in software*

---

## 11. Beforehand cushioning
Compensate for the relatively low reliability of an object by preparing emergency means in advance.

*Examples: backup systems; redundant servers; airbags; insurance; autosave*

---

## 12. Equipotentiality
Change the operating conditions so that an object need not be raised or lowered; eliminate the need to change potential energy.

*Examples: adjustable-height workbenches; car pit in garage; lock-free data structures*

---

## 13. The other way round (Inversion)
Invert the action: instead of cooling, heat; instead of moving the object, move the environment; turn the object upside down.

*Examples: inverted umbrella; freeze-drying instead of heat-drying; pull instead of push*

---

## 14. Spheroidality / Curvature
Move from flat surfaces to spherical ones; from linear to rotational motion; use rollers, balls, spirals.

*Examples: ball bearings; rounded corners in UI to reduce visual stress; helical gears*

---

## 15. Dynamics
Make a system or its environment adjustable for optimal performance at each stage; divide an object into parts capable of moving relative to each other.

*Examples: adjustable car seats; dynamic pricing; responsive web design; flexible manufacturing*

---

## 16. Partial or excessive action
If 100% of a desired effect is hard to achieve, use slightly more or slightly less to simplify the problem.

*Examples: overfill and wipe off excess; approximate algorithms; spray more paint and sand down*

---

## 17. Another dimension
Move objects in two- or three-dimensional space; use multi-layered arrangements; tilt or reorient.

*Examples: multi-story parking; 3D printing; stacking chips in processors; QR codes*

---

## 18. Mechanical vibration
Cause an object to oscillate or vibrate; increase frequency (up to ultrasonic); use resonance frequency; use piezoelectric vibration.

*Examples: ultrasonic cleaning; vibrating concrete during pouring; noise-canceling headphones*

---

## 19. Periodic action
Replace continuous action with periodic (impulse) action; if already periodic, change the frequency; use pulses between impulses.

*Examples: flashing warning lights (more attention than steady); pulsed laser; interval training*

---

## 20. Continuity of useful action
Carry on work continuously; make all parts work at full load all the time; eliminate idle and intermediate motions.

*Examples: rotary engine vs. piston engine; continuous casting in steelmaking; streaming vs. batch*

---

## 21. Skipping / Rushing through
Conduct a process or certain stages at high speed to reduce harmful side effects.

*Examples: high-speed drilling to avoid heat; flash pasteurization; fast-forward through ads*

---

## 22. Blessing in disguise (Convert harm to benefit)
Use harmful factors to obtain a positive effect; eliminate a harmful effect by combining it with another harmful effect; amplify a harmful factor until it ceases to be harmful.

*Examples: use waste heat for heating; turn noise into a signal; use vibration for mixing*

---

## 23. Feedback
Introduce feedback; if feedback already exists, change its magnitude or influence.

*Examples: thermostats; spell-checkers; A/B testing; real-time monitoring dashboards*

---

## 24. Intermediary
Use an intermediate object to carry, transmit, or facilitate an action; temporarily connect the object with another (easily removable) one.

*Examples: catalyst; proxy server; middleman in negotiation; transfer paper in printing*

---

## 25. Self-service
Make an object serve itself by performing auxiliary and repair functions; use waste resources, energy, or substances.

*Examples: self-cleaning oven; self-sharpening blades; self-healing materials; auto-update software*

---

## 26. Copying
Use simpler, inexpensive copies instead of expensive or fragile originals; replace physical objects with optical copies or images; move from visible to infrared or ultraviolet copies.

*Examples: virtual prototypes; digital twins; flight simulators; video conferencing*

---

## 27. Cheap short-living (Disposable)
Replace an inexpensive object with less expensive ones (sacrificing some quality); use disposable items to avoid cost of cleaning or repair.

*Examples: disposable medical instruments; single-use packaging; throwaway prototypes*

---

## 28. Mechanics substitution
Replace a mechanical system with optical, acoustic, thermal, or olfactory systems.

*Examples: optical sensors instead of mechanical switches; ultrasound instead of X-ray; touchscreen instead of physical buttons*

---

## 29. Pneumatics and hydraulics
Use gas or liquid parts instead of solid parts; use inflatable and hydraulic elements, air cushions, hydrostatic and hydro-reactive elements.

*Examples: pneumatic tires; hydraulic brakes; inflatable packaging; air bearings*

---

## 30. Flexible shells and thin films
Use flexible shells and thin films instead of three-dimensional structures; isolate the object from the environment using flexible shells and thin films.

*Examples: bubble wrap; protective coatings; graphene sheets; plastic wrap as barrier*

---

## 31. Porous materials
Make an object porous or use supplementary porous elements; if already porous, fill the pores with a useful substance.

*Examples: aerogel insulation; porous bone implants; activated carbon filters; foam padding*

---

## 32. Color changes
Change the color of an object or its environment; change the transparency of an object; use colored additives to observe otherwise invisible objects or processes; use luminescent tracers or markers.

*Examples: pH indicators; thermal imaging; color-coded cables; UV-reactive dye for leak detection*

---

## 33. Homogeneity
Make objects interacting with a given object of the same material (or material with identical properties).

*Examples: ice tools that cut ice (no contamination); biodegradable packaging; same-material welds*

---

## 34. Discarding and recovering
Make elements that have fulfilled their function disappear (dissolve, evaporate) or modify them directly during operation; restore consumable parts during operation.

*Examples: dissolving sutures; consumable electrodes; self-regenerating catalysts; cache eviction*

---

## 35. Parameter changes
Change an object's physical state (solid/liquid/gas); change concentration or consistency; change the degree of flexibility; change temperature or volume.

*Examples: freeze to transport, then thaw; change viscosity for different uses; shape-memory alloys*

---

## 36. Phase transitions
Use phenomena occurring during phase transitions (e.g., volume changes, heat absorption/release).

*Examples: steam power (water→steam); heat pipes using evaporation/condensation; dry ice cooling*

---

## 37. Thermal expansion
Use thermal expansion or contraction of materials; use materials with different coefficients of thermal expansion.

*Examples: bimetallic strip thermostats; shrink-fit assembly; thermal expansion joints in bridges*

---

## 38. Accelerated oxidation
Replace common air with oxygen-enriched air; replace enriched air with pure oxygen; use ionized oxygen or ozone; replace ozonized oxygen with ozone; replace ozone with other powerful oxidants.

*Examples: oxygen-enriched combustion; ozone water purification; plasma cutting*

---

## 39. Inert atmosphere
Replace a normal environment with an inert one; carry out the process in a vacuum.

*Examples: argon welding; nitrogen-flushed food packaging; vacuum desiccation*

---

## 40. Composite materials
Transition from homogeneous to composite (multi-component) materials.

*Examples: fiberglass; carbon fiber; concrete reinforced with rebar; multi-layer circuit boards*
